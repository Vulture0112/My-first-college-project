💡 What I Learned

  • The Power of Feature Hierarchy & Transfer Learning: By leveraging a pre-trained ResNet-50 network, we utilized universal early-level visual features (like edges, contrast gradients, and point-like blobs) that transfer seamlessly from natural images to astronomical data, bypassing the need to train a complex architecture from scratch.
  • Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA): Implementing Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) demonstrated that we can achieve exceptional classification accuracy (94.77%) while training only a tiny fraction (1.6%) of the network's parameters—a 98.4% parameter reduction with only a negligible accuracy drop.
  • Model Interpretability via Grad-CAM: Using Grad-CAM visualizations confirmed that our deep learning model bases its predictions on authentic physical structures (spiral arm curvature, central elliptical bulges, and tidal disruption tails) rather than background artifacts or dataset shortcuts.
  • Error Analysis & Morphological Overlap: Evaluating the confusion matrix revealed that the model most frequently confuses mergers and spirals, which makes physical sense as interacting galaxy systems display disturbed tidal arms closely mimicking normal spiral structures.
  • Observational Robustness: Stress-testing the network against data corruption showed that atmospheric seeing (blur) degrades classification performance much faster than CCD sensor noise, mirroring real-world observational constraints in astronomy.
